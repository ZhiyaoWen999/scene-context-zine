# Calibration Examples

Use these examples as decision patterns, not templates. Replace every observation with evidence from the actual source.

## Contents

## 1. Brown café interior

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: seated person, white tabletop, cup, window, pendant light.
- Spatial invariant: person at table with cold window light behind or beside them.
- Context-bearing regions: walnut-brown wall, translucent window, tabletop plane.
- Palette: walnut background; warm tan mid-tone; near-black furniture; amber interior light; muted green plant accent.
- Materials: wood, white laminate or stone, glass, ceramic.
- Density: layered but calm.
- Preserve: cup, light/window temperature contrast, table edge, wall color.
- Compress: repeated chairs and minor shelf objects.
- Omit: capture noise and redundant reflections.

### Best route

Choose Documentary Editorial first. Use raw-photo or material-continuation field, native palette, and light abstraction. Keep 75–90% photography.

If a margin replaces part of the room, continue the walnut material and one table/window edge into it. Brown color or paper grain alone is not enough.

### Do not do

Do not replace walnut with cream stock, turn the window into blank paper, or add cobalt/red merely for contrast.

## 2. Cool white gallery

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: person, artwork or architectural opening, cool white wall, polished floor line.
- Spatial invariant: human scale relative to wall or artwork.
- Context-bearing region: broad cool-white plane with subtle shadow and floor transition.
- Palette: cool white base; soft gray mid-tone; dark figure anchor; neutral daylight; source artwork accent if present.
- Materials: painted plaster, concrete or polished floor, glass.
- Density: sparse and ordered.

### Best route

Choose Documentary Editorial or Graphic Scene Poster. Use source-tone, not warm cream. Preserve shadow gradation even if the output remains flat.

Translate the wall into a cool value-continuation field that retains the real shadow break, floor line, or architectural opening; do not equate “white” with empty.

### Do not do

Do not interpret all white as empty paper. The exact white temperature, edge, and shadow carry the architecture.

## 3. Dense lived-in room

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: resident, desk or bed, stacked books, specific window/door, one personal collection.
- Context-bearing regions: shelves, floor storage, cables, textiles, or wall arrangements.
- Palette: derive from actual room; do not normalize.
- Materials: mixed wood, plastic, paper, fabric, electronics.
- Density: crowded, improvised, used.
- Preserve: object clusters that reveal use and personality.
- Compress: repeated book spines, cables, clothes, and containers into grouped rhythms.
- Omit: only accidental capture artifacts.

### Best route

Choose Multi-frame Joiner when several clusters matter, or Documentary Editorial when spatial continuity matters more. Use native palette and light abstraction.

Use shelf bands, cable direction, book-spine rhythm, or textile folds as connective information between fragments. Do not replace lived-in clusters with generic decorative marks.

### Do not do

Do not stage, declutter, beautify, or convert the room into aspirational minimalism. Do not treat all small objects as noise.

## 4. Night storefront

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: sign, doorway, person or vehicle, lit window, pavement reflection.
- Spatial invariant: sign-to-entry and light-to-street relationship.
- Context-bearing regions: façade color, illuminated interior, dark street edge.
- Palette: deep street base; warm or colored window; near-black anchor; mixed artificial light; sign accent.
- Materials: glass, metal shutter, wet asphalt, tile, painted sign.
- Density: layered, high contrast.

### Best route

Choose Graphic Scene Poster or Documentary Editorial. Use dark-stock or raw-photo. Amplify the existing sign color; do not introduce a new hue unless needed.

Let dark fields retain the real pavement reflection, shutter rhythm, or sign contour. A black stock area without those cues loses the street.

### Do not do

Do not lift the whole scene onto pale paper, wash out blacks, or add invented neon text.

## 5. Mountain or coastal travel scene

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: landmark or ridge, person/vehicle, shoreline/path, sky opening, distinctive structure.
- Spatial invariant: landmark scale and position relative to horizon or path.
- Context-bearing regions: sky/terrain boundary, material of stone/water/snow, local building or sign.
- Palette: source sky/terrain base, atmosphere mid-tone, dark landform, weather light, real clothing/sign accent.
- Density: expansive or weathered.

### Best route

Choose Graphic Scene Poster with the anchored synthesis for a landmark-led source; choose Multi-frame Joiner for a travel-memory source with several points of attention. Use material-continuation and source amplification. At balanced strength, keep 45–65% truthful photography, reserve at least 25% for source-derived graphic fields, and combine a content-derived grid with a crop/scale shift plus one boundary crossing. Use bold strength when the user explicitly wants strong differentiation and the landmark/reflection relationship is robust.

Map sky and terrain fields back to the actual cloud bands, ridge contour, rock rhythm, shoreline, or reflection axis. Do not use a generic sun disc, arrow, or abstract mountain triangle as a substitute.

### Do not do

Do not make the photo a tiny sticker on generic paper. Do not flatten the landmark into an unrelated geometric symbol. Do not return the full-frame source with only sky halftone, saturation, grain, or color grading.

## 6. Moving street scene

### Scene Contract pattern

- Identity anchors: moving person/vehicle, storefront or station element, road marking, directional sign.
- Spatial invariant: movement direction, gaze, path, and overlap.
- Context-bearing regions: street surface, façade rhythm, transit infrastructure.
- Palette: preserve street temperature and real signage/vehicle colors.
- Density: dynamic, crowded, directional.

### Best route

Choose Multi-frame Joiner with three to seven source crops or Graphic Scene Poster with one strong diagonal. Keep the moving subject intact in the largest fragment.

Carry motion through the real road marking, rail, gaze, stride, façade rhythm, or reflection direction. Do not add arrows when the source already contains a directional structure.

### Do not do

Do not clone the person inconsistently, invent future positions, or rotate fragments until the street becomes spatially impossible.
